Getting started

What is a rider?

A rider is a shared list for a trip, a gig or an event — what needs bringing, who is doing what, and what it all costs. The name comes from touring, where a rider is the list an artist sends ahead. Make one, invite the people coming, and everyone sees the same list.

What’s the difference between the shared and private lists?

The shared list is the rider itself: everyone you have invited can see it and, unless the rider is set to need approval, change it.

The private list is yours alone. Nobody else can see it — not other members, not the person who created the rider. Use it for the things only you have to remember. You can move a private item onto the shared list later if you want to.

How do I invite someone?

Open the rider, tap the person icon, and issue a pass. A pass is a code you send to one person or a small group; they enter it in “Join / View rider” and they are in. You choose whether each pass gives full access or view-only.

Issue a separate pass for each person or group. That way you can see who used what, and you can revoke one without affecting anybody else.

Why did my item say “Needs approval”?

The rider is set so that items suggested by members are approved before they appear on the shared list. Nothing is lost — your suggestion is waiting for someone with full access to accept it. The person who created the rider chooses this setting, and it can be changed.

What can a view-only guest do?

They can see the whole rider, see who is coming, and answer for themselves — including how many people they are bringing. They cannot add, edit or delete items, suggest items, or comment. It is the right level for somebody who needs to know the plan but is not helping build it.

Using a rider

What does “on the list” mean?

Each item tracks who is in and who is out. Marking yourself in says “count me in for this” — useful when an item only applies to some of you. The counts under an item show how many are in and how many are out.

Being on the list for an item is separate from whether you are paying for it; that is set in the cost split.

How do I put a private item on the shared list?

On your private list, use “Add to shared”. The item moves across and everybody on the rider can see it from then on. It moves rather than copies, so it leaves your private list.

Can I assign an item to someone?

Yes. Open the item and set who it is for. They get a notification, and anybody can filter the rider by who an item is assigned to. Assigning is a note about who is handling something; it does not change who is paying for it.

What is a decision, and how do I undo one?

When an item has been discussed and you have settled on an answer, you can lock that answer in as the decision. Press and hold the control rather than tapping it, so it cannot happen by accident. The decision then shows on the item, with who made it and when.

Undo is on the same item and puts it back to undecided.

How do comments work?

Every item has its own thread. Comments are visible to everyone on the rider except view-only guests, who cannot post. Mentioning somebody notifies them.

What are the calendar and map views for?

Calendar shows every item that has a date, laid out by day, which is the fastest way to see whether a day is overloaded. Map shows every item that has a location. Items with no date or no location do not appear in those views — they are still on the list.

Notifications

What gets me a notification?

Nine things, all of them somebody else acting on something of yours:

  • somebody assigns you an item
  • somebody proposes an item on a rider where you review proposals
  • your proposal is approved, or declined
  • somebody puts you in a cost split — the notification carries the amount
  • somebody records a settlement involving you — with the amount and which way it went
  • somebody joins a rider you are on
  • somebody comments on an item you are following
  • somebody mentions you in a comment

You are never notified about your own actions. Assigning an item to yourself, approving your own proposal or settling your own share produces nothing, because you were there.

Where do I see them?

The bell, which is on your rider list and on every rider. The number on it is how many you have not read yet.

The bell is for your whole account, not one rider. It collects everything from every rider you are on, and each line names the rider it came from — so you do not have to go looking through riders to find out what changed.

Tapping a line opens the thing it is about. If you are already on that rider, the item opens straight away.

How do I clear them?

One at a time, swipe the line to the left, or use the × on it.

For all of them at once, the ⋮ button at the top of the list gives you Mark all read, which empties the number on the bell but keeps the lines, and Clear all, which removes them. Clear all asks you to confirm first.

Nothing disappears on its own — a notification stays until you clear it. The list holds your forty most recent.

Somebody says they never got notified

Three things it is usually not, and one it usually is.

A view-only guest is not notified about proposals, because they cannot review them. Nobody is notified about their own action. And if they are reading the same rider you are, there is nothing to tell them about.

Otherwise: a notification that fails to send never stops the thing that caused it. That is deliberate — an item you added is added whether or not anyone could be told. So a missing notification does not mean a missing change; check the rider itself before redoing anything.

Can I get these on my phone when the app is closed?

There is a Push notifications switch under Menu → Profile. It is per device: turning it on for your phone does not turn it on for your laptop, and each one has to be switched on where you want the alerts.

If you have blocked notifications for RiderMe in your browser, the switch cannot undo that — only you can, in your browser's settings for this site. The screen says so when that is what has happened.

Whether an alert reaches a locked phone depends on your phone and your browser, not on RiderMe. The bell inside the app is the reliable record; push is a convenience on top of it.

Offline & syncing

My change says “waiting” — is it saved?

Yes. RiderMe keeps changes you make with no signal and sends them as soon as you are back online, including if you close the app or your phone shuts it down in between. “Waiting” means the app is holding it for you and nothing needs doing.

The only message that asks something of you is “didn’t save”, which means the change did not happen and you will need to make it again.

It says “showing this rider as it was 4 hours ago”

You are offline, and this is the copy saved on your device from the last time the rider loaded. The time is how old that copy is, so you can judge whether to trust it — somebody may have changed something since.

You can still add and edit. Those changes wait, and send when you reconnect.

I added something offline and now I can’t see it

Two possibilities. If it is on the list with a note that changes are waiting, it is safe and will send.

If the rider itself is empty, the copy on your device was never saved — that happens when the rider has not been opened on this device recently — and anything you added has nowhere to appear until you reconnect.

Which riders work offline?

The last five you opened. Open a rider once while you have signal and it stays readable without one. If you are going somewhere without a connection, open the riders you will want before you go.

Costs & settling up

Who is included when a cost is split?

Whoever is selected on that item’s split — which you choose per item, and which is not the same as everybody on the rider. Somebody can be on the rider, and on the list for an item, and still not be paying for it.

Someone is bringing people — how does that change the split?

If somebody has said they are bringing others, their share is multiplied by the size of their party. Four people splitting something where one of them brings a guest is five shares, not four, and the person who brought the guest pays two of them.

Why did my share change after someone settled?

A settlement is recorded against the split as it stood at that moment, and it stays at that amount. If the split changes afterwards — somebody joins it, or the total moves — everyone else’s share is recalculated, but the settled amount does not move.

This is deliberate: a payment somebody has already made should not silently change size.

I paid someone back — how do I record it?

In Settle up, either of you can record it. The person who owes taps “I’ve paid my share”; the person who fronted the money can tap “Mark as paid to …” against them.

It is recorded straight away — there is no confirmation step and nothing waits on the other person.

What currency are the totals in?

Each cost is entered in whatever currency it was paid in, and converted into the rider’s currency when you enter it. That converted figure is then fixed — if the exchange rate moves later, the number on the item does not.

Access & passes

Someone says their passcode doesn’t work

Usually one of three things. The pass has already been claimed by as many people as it was issued for. The pass was revoked. Or they were removed from the rider, which destroys the pass they joined with.

Issue them a new pass. That is the only way to be sure the code being honoured is one you meant to give out.

I removed someone — can they come back?

Not with their old code. Removing somebody destroys the pass they joined with, so it stops working for them and for anybody else you sent it to. If you want them back, issue a new pass.

Why can’t I remove someone?

Only the person who created the rider can remove people.

Account, riders & privacy

What happens to a rider when the trip is over?

Nothing automatic. When you are done with a rider you can archive it, and it moves out of your main list into Archived, further down the same screen. Nothing is deleted and you can open it whenever you like.

Leaving a rider vs removing it from my list vs deleting it

Three different things.

Removing it from your list takes it off your screen. Everybody else’s rider is untouched.

Leaving takes you off the rider itself — the others see you go. It does not settle anything you owe or are owed, so sort that out first.

Deleting removes the rider for everyone, permanently. Only the person who created it can.

How do I delete my account?

There are instructions at riderme.app/delete-account, and you can start it from inside the app under Menu → Security. Deleting your account removes you from every rider you are on and deletes what is stored about you.

What do you store about me?

The privacy policy at riderme.app/privacy sets out what is collected, which other services are involved, and what you can ask for.
